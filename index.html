How to learn a board game

Rulebooks are written to be complete, and that makes them a poor way to learn. A complete rulebook lists components first and covers every edge case. A learner needs to know the purpose first. These eight questions, in this order, are what every sheet answers.

Purpose before procedure

A rule you understand the reason for is a rule you remember. Learn how you win and how the game ends before you learn what to do on a turn.

Learn in layers

The 30-second layer gets you playing. The 5-minute layer makes you competent. Save everything else for when it comes up. Every sheet has a toggle for this.

Show one turn, then play

A single concrete turn, played face-up and narrated, teaches more than ten minutes of explanation. Every sheet includes one.

Find the key decision

Every good game has one core dilemma. Once you see it, the rules stop looking arbitrary: they are what creates that tension.

Anchor to what you know

“It’s like Catan, but with cards” is a huge shortcut. The similarity map shows which games share mechanisms, so you can learn new ones by analogy.

The first game is practice

Play open-handed, let people take back moves, and don’t count the score too seriously. You are learning a system, not trying to win.
